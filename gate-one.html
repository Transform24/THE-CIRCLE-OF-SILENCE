Tool Two

The Wound Profile

She who has been stopped carries a wound with a name.
Which one is yours?

The Guilty Giver
She pours into everyone. She never stops to be filled.

You were made to give — and giving is holy. But what has broken you is not giving. It is giving from a place that was never refilled. The Guilty Giver carries others' weight as if it were a debt she owes. It is not. You were not made to pour from empty. The wound is not your love — it is the lie that love requires depletion.

"Behold, thou desirest truth in the inward parts: and in the hidden part thou shalt make me to know wisdom."

Psalm 51:6 — KJV

The Depleted Survivor
She has survived so much. She does not know how to rest.

You are still standing — and that is a testimony. But survival became your posture, and now rest feels like danger. The Depleted Survivor has learned to push through everything. What she has not yet learned is that pushing through was never the goal. Rest is not a reward you earn. It is a grace He extends — and He is extending it now.

"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

Matthew 11:28 — KJV

The Striving Achiever
She performs. She produces. She cannot be still.

You have built things, carried things, moved things forward. And somewhere in the building, you lost the ability to simply be. The Striving Achiever fears that stillness means falling behind. But this season of stopping was not a failure. It was an interruption arranged by One who knows that you will not hear Him at full speed. Be still — not as punishment, but as invitation.

"Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth."

Psalm 46:10 — KJV

The Lost Wanderer
She has lost the thread. She no longer knows who she is.

Somewhere in the seasons of loss and change, you lost yourself. The roles you carried defined you — and when they shifted, you did not know what remained. The Lost Wanderer is not lost to God. She is in exactly the place where He does His deepest work: the wilderness. The path forward is not one you must find. It is one that will be spoken to you, if you will stop long enough to hear.

"And thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it, when ye turn to the right hand, and when ye turn to the left."

Isaiah 30:21 — KJV

The wound has a name. So does the healing.
